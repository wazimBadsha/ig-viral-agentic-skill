# Research and Virality Method

## Research brief
Specify niche, audience, geography, language, timeframe, format, objective, source types, and desired sample. Ask only for missing details that change the answer.

## Discovery
Use authorized search/browser tools and user-provided URLs. Sample multiple creators, formats, account sizes, and typical posts; avoid selecting only extreme outliers. Record creator, URL, date, topic, format, opening hook paraphrase, visible metrics, source provenance, search date, and missing data.

## Viral analysis
For each post assess:
- Hook clarity, specificity, curiosity, tension, novelty, audience relevance.
- Narrative setup, stakes, progression, payoff, resolution.
- Retention potential: pacing, information density, open loops, visual changes, transitions. Never claim actual retention without creator analytics.
- Psychology: relatability, identity, aspiration, surprise, humor, empathy, utility, social currency.
- Engagement: save motive, share motive, meaningful discussion, CTA.
- Production: framing, overlays, audio, captions, edits, visual proof, accessibility.

## Pattern synthesis
A pattern requires multiple independent examples. Report counts and sample size. Label findings as observed, correlation, or hypothesis. Include counterexamples and confidence. Do not infer causality from public counters.

## Original reconstruction
Translate abstract creative mechanics into new premises, wording, examples, visuals, and narrative. Never copy a source's script, distinctive phrasing, or shot-for-shot expression.

## Scoring
Use the score dimensions:
- Hook 20
- Audience relevance 20
- Shareability 15
- Save-worthiness 15
- Storytelling 10
- Retention potential 10
- Originality 10

Scores are heuristic editorial assessments, not predicted reach.
